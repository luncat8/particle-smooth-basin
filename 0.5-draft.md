# 0.5 options

Written at the end of 0.4.2, where the thermal bands became one solved heat field that
the renderer paints, the solver samples, and the heat view shows. Everything below is an
option, not a decision: each entry says what it would cost and what would have to be
measured to call it done.

## A. A heat map beads can write into

The field is currently a pure source: only the two bands set it. Beads could also push
their own temperature into the cells they occupy, so a cold crust chills the water it
sits in and a rising hot bead drags a warm wake behind it.

- grid stays at 48 cells; adding a per-cell write pass is O(beads) per frame, no new
  allocations, and the solve already runs on the same grid;
- the danger is feedback: a cold bed would cool the whole tank and then bond harder.
  The band target can stay the anchor (`HEAT_PULL` already pulls cells back), so the
  drift is bounded by the pull;
- measure: mean tank temperature over 30 s at the default and at `sparseRaft`, plus the
  cold-bead/bond counts the 0.4.2 worklog records as the baseline. A scene that slowly
  freezes or thaws against `HEAT_PULL` would show up there first.

## B. A paintable second source layer

In the heat view, dragging could paint a local heat source or sink instead of moving a
band line. The field would become the sum of the band profile and a small painted layer.

- the painted layer has to be part of the settings JSON (a coarse array, or a list of
  `[x, y, radius, strength]` stamps) or copy/paste stops reproducing a scene;
- renders for free in the existing heat view if the layer is added before the solve;
- the open question is the interaction with the band drag: the same view would need a
  mode switch — paint versus move a line — and the panel would need to say which;
- measure: solve cost with a painted layer (cells change every stroke, so no warm
  start), and whether the painted spot is stable under `HEAT_PULL`.

## C. Settle and archive the renderer

The heat wash is a 48-cell grid stretched over the window, which reads as a smooth
gradient at any DPR, but it is painted with `drawImage` scaling; a higher grid, or a
per-pixel canvas at DPR, would be a different cost class.

- the honest test is the browser matrix's `heat cap` case at DPR 1 and 2;
- if the wash looks banded at 1500 beads and DPR 2, raise `HEAT_GRID` before changing
  the draw path — the solve moves from 6 ms to 15 ms cold at 96 cells, and the frame
  budget still allows it.

## D. WebGPU gate

0.3.2 closed it: CPU/Canvas2D stayed inside budget by ~7× at the worst case. 0.4.2 adds
one full-frame scaled draw and a solved field; nothing here changes the verdict unless
the browser matrix says the heat view at the cap cannot hold 16.7 ms. Re-check after the
user's browser run, not before.

## E. Packaging the six couplings

The couplings are now two keys applied to a live bed. If they stay interesting, the next
step is a small table of scenes × couplings in `tests.html` (one row per combination
against the shipped scene) so a coupling that only works on `sparseRaft` is visible as
such. Cheap: it reuses the existing perf-case machinery, no new engine code.
