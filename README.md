# Hydrogel Basin

A file://-friendly particle basin experiment. Open `index.html` directly in a modern browser; there are no external libraries or build steps.

## Controls

- Drag beads to grab/toss; click the basin to add a bead; drag a dashed band line to move it. Hovering changes the cursor (grab over a bead, vertical resize over a line) but does not stir; hold/drag to apply the brush force.
- `SPACE` splashes the bed; `R` re-deals it using the current settings.
- **Thermal bands** are the basin's background, not a per-bead force: a cold band hangs from the top of the window and a hot band rises from the floor, and the two are solved once into a smooth signed source field around neutral `0.5`. Cells below `0.5` cool warmer beads toward the cold bath floor, cells above `0.5` heat beads toward `1`, and the clear middle of the map leaves beads to conduction. **Cold band end** (10–90% of the window) and **hot band start** (10–90%) place the bands, or drag the dashed lines drawn in the tank; **cold speed** (0–4×) and **hot speed** (0–3.5×) set how fast each half pulls, and **conduction** (0–2×) sets heat transfer between touching beads.
- **Heat lifts** (0–1) makes a hot bead lighter than a cold one: the effective density is scaled by `1 - lift·2·(T - 0.5)`. That is what lets a heated floor overturn the bed instead of only nudging neighbours through the size coupling.
- **Bond strength** (0.2–1) is the stiffness of a cold contact bond and **bond vs temperature** (1–3 slider, 0.5–6 paste) is how sharply that stiffness falls as the pair warms.
- **Thermal size** controls how temperature changes collision and drawn radius. At full positive response, the radius at temperature 1 is twice the radius at temperature 0; negative response reverses the effect.
- **Archimedes force vs size** is a signed exponent (-10 to +4 slider, -10 to +10 paste): buoyant lift scales with `(radius / typical radius)^value`. The lift multiplier is bounded to 1/8–8× so extreme settings remain usable.
- **Buoyancy strength** scales the Archimedes term (0–1.2× slider, 0–2× paste). At 0, beads feel gravity without buoyancy; 1× preserves the default density-based balance.
- **Scene preset** lists five scenes that re-deal the bed (convection by thermal lift with a negative or a positive size response, convection by heat, a filled balanced bed, and the 0.4.0 sparse raft) followed by six couplings that retune `size`, `arch`, and `buoyancy` on the live bed without a re-deal (hot shrinks · flow only, big beads float, convection with hot beads shrinking or growing, and stable layering with hot beads sinking or floating). Moving any control switches the select to **Custom sliders**.
- **Water level** (20–45% of the window height) moves the waterline without a re-deal; the bed re-floats around it.
- **Canvas coverage** (5–70% of summed typical particle-disc area) and **typical radius** (8–22 px) set the number and scale of beads on the next re-deal; the small caption under the coverage slider estimates the bead count and says when the particle cap limits it.
- **Average mass** scales inertia while the independent random density continues to determine buoyancy.
- **View** cycles density colours → the temperature palette → the **heat map** (the button, or keys `1`/`2`/`3`). The heat map draws the signed source field — cold blue from the top, hot amber from the floor, clear where the map is neutral — and dims the beads so the field reads through them.
- **Copy settings** writes every control as one JSON line (`fill`, `water`, `coldEnd` and the other percentage controls are 0–1 in the line and percentages in the panel; `view` travels too); **Paste settings** takes that line back, re-deals the bed and re-syncs the panel, and **Defaults** restores the shipped scene. Paste names the keys it ignores, and every key passes through the same setters the sliders use, so a hand-edited line cannot leave the world in a state the panel cannot describe.

The shipped scene is the dense convection bed: 40% coverage at a 14 px typical radius, waterline at 30%, the cold band down to 30% and heat from 75% to the floor. Thermal size is −1 and the Archimedes exponent is −10, so a hot bead shrinks hard and the little ones float: the cold top swells and bonds into a crust while the heated floor keeps turning over (~21 hot beads/s carried above mid-bed). After eight seconds at the defaults roughly a fifth of the bed is cold enough to bond and the top quarter of the tank reads ~0.34 against ~0.91 at the floor. Cold nearby beads form distance-constraint bonds that soften as the pair warms and thaw once warm or stretched beyond neighbour range.

## Development checks

Double-click `run_tests.py` (or `python3 run_tests.py` — no arguments needed). It runs the node checks, then serves this directory and opens `tests.html` in your browser, and both halves land in one log named `<version>-tests-<date-time>.log` under `experiments/logs/`, with the version read from `VERSION`. The server's root redirects to the tests page; the app itself stays at `/index.html`.

- **Node checks** — `experiments/smoke.js` (classic-script boot, all eleven presets, sliders, band drag, pointer cancel, DPR change, clipboard copy/paste/reset, `?profile=1`), `experiments/thermal.js` (thermal, pointer, wave-step, bond lifetime), `experiments/bench.js` (seeded throughput, link-capacity baseline and the Archimedes equilibrium of a lone bead), `experiments/thermal-zone.js` (band geometry and source-rate sweep: bond count, band temperatures, link churn, stir recovery), `experiments/settings.js` (copy/paste round trip, partial pastes, unknown keys, clamping, the heat-map field, 10 s stability of the pasted scene), `experiments/harness.js` (lint and sandbox run of the browser harness).
- **Browser tests** — `tests.html` runs its checks (boot, canvas pixels, invariants, pointer/splash, resize, thermal bounds, shipped-scene geometry and bond visibility, profiler) and a frame-profile matrix that always starts with the shipped scene, then ≈300/810/1500 beads × DPR 1/2 × temperature view, then a cold/high-link coupling, a stirred near-cap scene and the heat-map view at the cap. Every case reports p50/p95 for the frame callback, `stepWorld`, `render`, and the `requestAnimationFrame` interval.
- **GPU** — the environment line classifies the renderer (hardware versus SwiftShader/llvmpipe), and the `rAF p95` column is where raster and compositor cost shows up; there is no WebGPU path yet, so no GPU-versus-CPU parity case exists.
- **Results** — the page appends its section to the run log automatically when a run finishes and then shows `Saved ✓` on a disabled, highlighted button: the same log is never written twice (the server also rejects a repeated section, so two tabs cannot duplicate it). The button comes back as soon as a new line exists. `Copy log` copies the log. Opening `tests.html` directly (`file://`) works too — Save then downloads the file.

The server sends `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy`, so the page is cross-origin isolated and `performance.now()` keeps microsecond resolution instead of being coarsened to 0.1 ms. That is the only reason the runner is a server; `file://` timings still work but are coarser.

`index.html?profile=1` measures the real page (HUD included) on its own: after 300 frames the stats line and console show p50/p95 for frame, simulation, and render. The recorder lives in `js/profile.js` and is shared with `tests.html`; profiling is off unless requested and adds no per-frame allocation.

The engine is DOM-free and can be required directly by Node; `experiments/fake-dom.js` boots a page bundle in a VM for the node checks, which validates logic but never canvas pixels or real frame times.
