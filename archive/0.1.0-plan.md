# 0.1 hydrogel basin — base simulation

## goal

basin of water at 50% screen height filled ~70% with hydrogel beads of
random size and density: light beads float and get pushed out of the
water by crowding, heavy beads sink. smooth (squishy, non-bouncy)
collisions, small visual gaps between packed beads. interactive: grab
and toss beads, click to add, stir, splash, resettle.

## world

- rectangular glass tank: x 5.5%..94.5% of width, floor at 93.5% height,
  open top; soft ceiling far above the rim so tossed beads fall back.
- waterline fixed at 50% of screen height (brief: "typical water level").
- 1-D wave field (~160 spring columns) along the waterline for ripples.
- resize remaps beads into the new tank geometry (debounced).

## particles

flat typed-array storage (SoA), stable indices — beads are appended,
never removed or reordered:

- x, y, px, py, vx, vy (Float64), r, dens, invM, frac, alpha (Float64),
  pal (Uint8 → sprite palette)
- radius: rBar-scaled big/small mix (mean area ~3.226 rBar²); rBar sized
  so TARGET_PARTICLES beads cover FILL_FRAC of the water body
- density mix: 50% floats (0.45–0.95), 15% near-neutral (0.95–1.08),
  35% sinks (1.1–2.1)
- 2D mass: m ∝ dens·r² (pi cancels in all weightings)

## physics

fixed accumulator at SUBSTEP = 1/120 s, MAX_STEPS per frame. per substep:

1. forces: gravity vs archimedes `a = g·(1 − f/ρ)` where f is the true
   submerged area fraction of the disc (exact circular segment, not a
   smoothstep); viscous drag `1/(1 + k·h)` with k = air + water·f²;
   pointer grab spring or stir brush; integrate positions.
2. contacts: uniform grid (typed arrays, cell = 2·rMax), one visit per
   unordered pair, then soft mass-weighted overlap projection
   (CONTACT_SOFT, CONTACT_ITER iterations) + wall clamps.
3. velocities derived from position deltas (PBD, no restitution ->
   calm), then normal-velocity damping on touching pairs
   (CONTACT_DAMP = 1, hydrogel is inelastic).
4. beads near the surface kick the wave field -> ripples, and pay the
   opposite impulse (radiation damping) so the coupling drains bobbing
   energy instead of pumping it.

buoyancy reads the flat tank level, never the wave height: feedback from
a local meniscus resonates at the bead's bobbing frequency and pumps the
oscillation forever (measured; see findings). archimedes is defined
against the undisturbed level anyway. waves stay visual + act as the
energy sink.

## solver seam for freeze links (0.2)

the relaxation loop in `solveContacts` is the single place constraints
are resolved; `world.pairRF` widens the pair scan beyond contact range
for link discovery. distance constraints (links) join the same iteration
loop. stable bead indices keep link endpoints valid.

## rendering

- bead sprites pre-rendered once (12-color palette: light/deep by
  density), drawImage per bead with per-bead alpha
- drawn radius = RENDER_SHRINK · collision radius -> visual gaps
- gradients (bg, glow, water, floor) cached at resize; no per-frame
  allocations, no string building in the frame loop
- water body under beads + tint pass over beads; wave polyline drawn as
  glow + crisp line; glass tank outline; static waterline label

## files

- `js/engine.js` — physics, pure (no DOM), require-able under node
- `js/render.js` — sprites, cached assets, frame drawing
- `js/app.js` — canvas fit, input, HUD, rAF loop
- `index.html` — page shell, classic script tags, file:// friendly
- `experiments/bench.js` — node bench + physics sanity checks

## performance budget

~650 beads at 60 fps with large headroom (target: physics < 1 ms/frame
in node at 1280×800; render bound by drawImage throughput). zero
steady-state heap allocation per frame.
